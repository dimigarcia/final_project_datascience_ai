# Deliverable 1 – Initial Product Ideas

## Idea 1 – Campaign Context AI: Context-Aware Promotion Impact Analysis for Independent Artists

### Problem it addresses

Independent artists use social media to promote releases, concerts and other activity, but campaign metrics such as views, likes or engagement are usually separated from outcomes such as streams, organic followers or sales.

This makes it difficult to determine whether a campaign was followed by a meaningful change in the outcomes that matter to the artist. Attribution is further complicated by simultaneous events such as releases, concerts, playlist placements, press coverage or paid advertising.

The proposed product would combine campaign data, artist outcomes and contextual events in a single analytical system. An AI assistant would allow artists to describe relevant events in natural language and convert them into structured information that can be incorporated into the analysis.

### Motivation for the project

Independent artists and small music organisations have limited time and promotional budgets, so understanding which activities are associated with meaningful results can improve resource allocation.

The project combines AI-assisted information extraction, time-series analysis and data visualisation in a practical decision-support tool.

Prospective data could come from artist-owned analytics and exports from platforms such as Spotify for Artists, Instagram Insights, TikTok analytics, YouTube Studio or Bandcamp, complemented by manually supplied information on releases, concerts and promotional activity.

### Who it impacts

The main users would be independent musicians managing their own promotion.

It could also benefit artist managers, small labels and promotion teams that need to evaluate campaigns without access to expensive specialist analytics services.

### Why it has value

The product would help users evaluate campaigns using downstream outcomes rather than engagement metrics alone.

By incorporating contextual events, it could provide a more realistic interpretation of changes in streams, followers or sales and support better decisions about where to invest promotional effort and budget.

The analysis would estimate campaign-associated changes while making clear that observational data do not necessarily establish definitive causal effects.

### Minimum viable product (MVP) scope

The minimum viable product (MVP) would focus on one artist using exported daily or weekly analytics.

The artist would provide contextual events through a conversational AI interface, while a time-series or event-based model would compare campaign periods with changes in selected outcomes and account for known concurrent events and possible lagged effects.

## Idea 2 – Housing Pressure: Mapping the Rent–Income Gap

### Problem it addresses

Housing-crisis discussions often focus on rental prices alone. However, the real burden of housing depends on how those costs compare with household income.

Rent and income statistics are commonly reported separately, making it harder to identify areas where housing costs are becoming increasingly disconnected from residents' economic capacity.

The proposed project would combine rental and income data to create a geographically and temporally comparable indicator of housing-affordability pressure.

### Motivation for the project

Housing affordability is a major social and economic issue linked to inequality, residential mobility and household welfare.

Combining existing public datasets could make the problem easier to interpret and reveal whether affordability pressures are concentrated in particular areas or worsening over time.

Prospective data could be obtained from public sources such as Barcelona Open Data, the Catalan Statistical Institute (Idescat), the Spanish National Statistics Institute (INE) and other municipal or regional housing datasets.

### Who it impacts

The main beneficiaries would be tenants and prospective renters seeking to understand affordability differences between areas.

The results could also be useful to housing organisations, journalists, researchers and public institutions working on housing policy.

### Why it has value

The product would improve transparency by evaluating rents relative to income rather than showing housing prices in isolation.

It could identify neighbourhoods where rents are increasing faster than incomes, reveal spatial inequalities and support evidence-based discussion of housing policy and affordability.

### MVP scope

The MVP would focus on Barcelona and use districts or neighbourhoods as the geographical unit.

It would combine rental-price and income indicators over several years, construct a transparent affordability measure, and visualise the results through maps and time trends.

## Idea 3 – Research Impact Lens: Transparent Academic Impact Profiles

### Problem it addresses

Academic research impact is often communicated through raw indicators such as citation counts, journal metrics or researcher-level indices. These measures can be misleading when interpreted without context because citation practices vary across disciplines and older publications have had more time to accumulate citations.

Relevant information about impact and research openness is also distributed across different platforms, making it difficult to obtain a clear and transparent view of a researcher's outputs.

The proposed product would create contextualised research-impact profiles that complement conventional metrics with interpretable comparisons.

### Motivation for the project

Research evaluation influences academic careers, funding, recruitment and the communication of scientific impact. More transparent indicators could therefore support better-informed individual and institutional decisions.

The project would provide an opportunity to apply data integration, bibliometric analysis, normalisation and visualisation while maintaining a strong focus on interpretability.

Prospective data could be obtained from established scholarly databases and APIs such as OpenAlex, Crossref, ORCID and Unpaywall, which provide publication metadata, citation information, authorship data and open-access indicators.

### Who it impacts

The main users would be academics and research groups seeking to understand and communicate their publication impact more clearly.

Research departments, universities and evaluators could also benefit from more contextualised information than raw citation totals alone.

### Why it has value

The product could improve transparency by showing how research metrics depend on factors such as publication age and disciplinary context.

It could help academics identify publications that perform strongly relative to comparable work and reduce reliance on isolated or poorly contextualised indicators.

### MVP scope

The MVP would analyse a limited set of researchers or publications using publicly available scholarly metadata.

It would present conventional citation measures alongside simple field- and publication-year-adjusted comparisons and open-access status, focusing on transparent interpretation rather than attempting to create a universal measure of research quality.
